Tirzepatide
the dual agonist

One molecule, two gut-hormone switches — GIP and GLP-1. Here is what the trials actually show, separated from the hype.

Quick read

Tirzepatide (LY3298176) is Eli Lilly’s once-weekly dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonist. In the 72-week SURMOUNT-1 trial the 15 mg dose averaged 20.9% weight loss, and the 10 mg dose nearly matched it at 19.5% — most of the benefit arriving well before the top dose. Nausea and diarrhea were the common trade-offs. Scroll for the full picture.

01The molecule

What tirzepatide actually is

Tirzepatide (development code LY3298176) is a 39-amino-acid synthetic peptide built as a GIP analog that also switches on the GLP-1 receptor — with a fatty-acid tail that keeps it circulating for about five days.

Highlighted beads mark engineered residues; the gradient runs GLP-1 sky → GIP teal.

GIP-first DESIGN

Engineered as a GIP analog that also activates GLP-1 — the reverse emphasis of earlier drugs, which were GLP-1 analogs first. That ordering turned out to matter.

~5-day HALF-LIFE

The C20 diacid tail hitches onto albumin (99% bound), shielding the peptide from clearance. Five days is what makes once-weekly injections work.

Two BRAND NAMES

Mounjaro for type 2 diabetes, Zepbound for chronic weight management and sleep apnea with obesity. Same molecule inside — different label, indication, and price.

Plain EnglishThink of it as a lab-built copy of two of your gut’s “I’m full” hormones, fused into one long-lasting molecule — with a fatty tail that lets it hitchhike on a blood protein, so a single weekly dose keeps working for days.
02Origin story

The twincretin bet

Tirzepatide wasn’t discovered by accident. It was the product of a deliberate bet: that GIP — a gut hormone most researchers had written off — could be rehabilitated and paired with GLP-1 inside a single molecule.

Simplified timeline. The SURPASS diabetes program ran 2019–2021; SURMOUNT obesity trials followed.

The TWINCRETIN IDEA

Early work showed one peptide could hit both incretin receptors — high affinity for GIP and GLP-1, negligible glucagon activity. In animals it beat single-hormone drugs on glucose, weight, and food intake.

Built FROM GIP UP

The starting sequence was human GIP itself — with engineered residues borrowed from GLP-1 and exendin-4, plus DPP-4–resistant building blocks so the body can’t shred it in minutes.

GIP’s REHABILITATION

GIP had lost its insulin effect in diabetes patients and was considered a dead end. Pairing it with GLP-1 brought it back to life — the dismissed hormone became the key ingredient.

Plain EnglishEveryone thought GIP was useless for diabetes — then someone tried it with GLP-1 instead of instead of it. The combination did what neither could alone, and a decade later it was the best-selling drug in its class.
03How it works

How it’s claimed to work

After you eat, your gut releases incretin hormones that tell the pancreas, brain, and stomach what to do. Tirzepatide flips two of those switches at once. Below: what’s solid versus what’s still inference.

PROVEN IN HUMANSmeasured in trialsCLAIMED / INFERREDlab logic, still being nailed down

Simplified: in the body the two receptors live on different cells and tissues; the drug reaches both.

GLP-1 — proven

The fullness signal. Released by the gut after meals. Slows how fast the stomach empties, tells the brain you’ve had enough, and nudges the pancreas to release insulin. This is the lever semaglutide pulls on its own — its effects are measured, not hypothetical.

GIP — proven + inferred

The amplifier. The gut’s other incretin. Proven: it boosts insulin release when blood sugar is high, and the drug’s weight loss exceeds GLP-1 alone. Inferred: researchers believe GIP signaling dampens the nausea GLP-1 causes and acts directly on appetite circuits — the exact wiring is still being mapped.

Who it’s for — on paper

MOUNJARO

Type 2 diabetes. Approved for adults with type 2 diabetes, alongside diet and exercise, to improve glycemic control. This was tirzepatide’s first approval and the indication with the deepest trial record (the SURPASS program).

ZEPBOUND · WEIGHT

Chronic weight management. Approved for adults with obesity (BMI ≥ 30), or overweight (BMI ≥ 27) with a weight-related condition such as high blood pressure or abnormal cholesterol — alongside a reduced-calorie diet and increased physical activity.

ZEPBOUND · SLEEP APNEA

Moderate-to-severe obstructive sleep apnea with obesity. The first drug ever approved for this use — the trials showed meaningfully fewer breathing interruptions per night, not just weight loss.

Labels describe populations, not people. Whether it’s appropriate for any individual — medical history, other medications, pregnancy plans, cost — is a clinician’s call, not a checklist on a web page.
Plain EnglishGLP-1 is the brake pedal on hunger; GIP is the skilled co-driver who makes the braking smoother and stronger. We know the car slows down (proven). We’re still learning exactly how the co-driver helps (inferred).
04Evidence check

The SURMOUNT program

Tirzepatide ran one of the largest obesity trial programs ever assembled. The flagship, SURMOUNT-1, randomized 2,539 adults with obesity (no diabetes) to 5, 10, or 15 mg weekly or placebo for 72 weeks — including a 20-week dose-escalation ramp.

SURMOUNT-1 · average weight change at 72 weeks

15 mg−20.9% average
10 mg−19.5% average
5 mg−15.0% average
Placebo−3.1% average
Treatment-regimen estimand (intention-to-treat, quitters counted). 91% on 15 mg lost ≥5%; 57% lost ≥20%. Fat mass fell roughly 3× faster than lean mass (33.9% vs 10.9%). The 10 and 15 mg results were, in the investigators’ words, “relatively similar.”
SURMOUNT-2

With type 2 diabetes. Weight loss ran smaller than in SURMOUNT-1 — diabetes blunts the effect — but blood-sugar control improved dramatically. Same drug, harder patient population.

SURMOUNT-3

After a lifestyle run-in. Participants first lost weight with intensive diet and exercise, then added tirzepatide — and kept losing. The drug stacks on top of lifestyle, not instead of it.

SURMOUNT-4

The stop test. After 36 weeks everyone was doing well; those switched to placebo regained ~14%. Obesity behaves like a chronic condition that needs ongoing treatment — not a course you finish.

SURMOUNT-5

Head-to-head vs semaglutide 2.4 mg. The first direct duel of the two giants — no cross-trial guesswork needed. Tirzepatide lost more weight.

SUMMIT

Obesity + heart failure (HFpEF). Fewer hospitalizations and cardiovascular events than placebo — the benefit reaches beyond the scale.

Single, double, triple — separate trials, not head-to-head

Each bar is that drug’s own flagship trial — different people, different years. Only SURMOUNT-5 was a true head-to-head.

Semaglutide −14.9% · ONE LEVER

STEP 1, 68 weeks. GLP-1 alone, pulled hard. The drug that proved the category could deliver double-digit loss.

Tirzepatide −20.9% · TWO LEVERS

SURMOUNT-1, 72 weeks. Adding GIP bought ~6 extra points — and the GIP arm may soften the nausea GLP-1 causes alone.

Retatrutide −28.3% · THREE LEVERS

TRIUMPH-1, 80 weeks. Adds a glucagon lever for another ~7 points. Still in late-stage trials — more efficacy, more unknowns.

Plain EnglishEach generation added one more hormone switch and gained roughly six to seven percentage points of weight loss. Tirzepatide is the middle generation — and the only one of the three you can currently get prescribed.
05What people say

Stories from online

Tirzepatide has one of the largest online patient communities of any drug. These are unverified anecdotes — useful for knowing what the experience feels like, useless as evidence. Every tile is stamped.

How to read this chapter: forums amplify extremes — spectacular wins and spectacular side effects. Quiet, average experiences rarely get posted. Treat every tile as one person’s story, not a prediction.
Unverified anecdote — the pulsing dot marks a paraphrased recurring theme from forums and social media: not verified, not evidence.

Food noise: gone

“It’s like someone turned down a radio I didn’t know was blasting. I used to plan my whole day around meals — what’s for lunch, is there a snack at 3, what’s for dinner. Now I get busy and realize at 3pm I haven’t thought about food once. I just… forget to snack. My pantry is full of things I bought on autopilot that I no longer want.”

Caveat: The quieting is widely reported and feels real for many — but it can fade after months, it doesn’t happen for everyone, and “not thinking about food” can slide into undereating if you’re not deliberate about protein and regular meals. Appetite suppression is a tool, not a meal plan.

The sulfur burps

“Week three and I am a human foghorn. Rotten-egg burps for hours after dinner, and my partner has started eating in the other room. Gas-X does nothing, antacids do nothing. It’s worse on days I eat anything fried or eat too fast — which, frankly, the drug is also making harder to do.”

Caveat: Tirzepatide’s infamous calling card — usually worst during dose escalation and often eases as the gut adapts. Smaller, lower-fat, slower meals help some people. But persistent vomiting, severe abdominal pain, dehydration, or inability to keep fluids down needs a clinician, not a forum thread.

Didn’t need to climb

“My doctor wanted to keep pushing me up the ladder toward 15. I held at 7.5 — down 45 lbs, labs perfect, almost no nausea. At that point more drug just wasn’t buying me more results, only more side effects. I’m not climbing any higher unless progress actually stalls.”

Caveat: Matches the trial pattern, where the lower doses delivered most of the benefit and side effects climbed with every step. But “enough” is personal — some people genuinely need higher doses — and holding or stopping escalation is a prescriber conversation, not a DIY decision.

Mounjaro vs Zepbound: same pen?

“Pharmacist told me they’re literally the same drug. My insurance covers Mounjaro and not Zepbound — or was it the other way around? I’ve spent six hours on hold across three phone calls to learn that the molecule is identical and the price difference is several hundred dollars a month. Make it make sense.”

Caveat: Correct — same molecule, different FDA labels and wildly different prices. Insurance logic is not pharmacology. If you’re navigating this, the relevant question for your prescriber and pharmacist is which label your plan covers, not which drug is “better.”

Stalled at 5 mg

“Lost 12 lbs in the first two months, then nothing — six weeks of the scale not moving a tenth of a pound. I was convinced it had stopped working. My prescriber moved me up one step and the scale woke back up within two weeks. The stall was the dose, not me.”

Caveat: Plateaus are normal on any dose, and escalation restarted loss in the trials too — but a six-week stall can also be water retention, muscle gain, or just normal noise. Chasing the scale upward has its own costs in side effects, so stalls deserve a real conversation before a dose change.

The hair-shedding scare

“Month four, clumps in the shower drain every morning. Nobody warned me — not my doctor, not the pharmacist, not the pamphlet. I was sure the drug was poisoning me. Then I learned it has a name, it happens after bariatric surgery too, and it usually grows back.”

Caveat: Rapid weight loss itself triggers telogen effluvium — the body sheds hair 2–4 months after a major stressor, including fast weight loss from any cause. It’s distressing but typically temporary. Persistent shedding, bald patches, or shedding with other symptoms still deserves a clinician’s eyes.

The itchy welt

“Every shot leaves a red lump the size of a quarter for three days — hot, itchy, impossible not to scratch. I’ve tried icing before and after, rotating sites, antihistamines. It always fades, but the three days after shot day I’m very aware of my thigh.”

Caveat: Injection-site reactions are listed, common, and usually mild — redness, itching, small lumps that resolve on their own. But spreading redness, increasing pain, warmth with fever, or lumps that harden and don’t fade are a different story and worth a call.

The cost cliff

“$1,100 a month without coverage. I did the math three times: staying on it costs more than my car payment. I’ve watched people in my support group go off it when a prior auth lapsed and regain everything within months. The drug works. The price is the disease now.”

Caveat: The most-reported “side effect” isn’t medical at all — access shapes every anecdote you’ll read, because the people posting are disproportionately the ones who could afford to stay on it. Any discussion of real-world outcomes that ignores cost is missing the biggest variable.

The prior-auth saga

“Approved, denied, appealed, denied again, peer-to-peer review, approved. Four months of paperwork, two crying phone calls, and one very patient pharmacist before the first shot. My doctor’s office has a full-time employee whose entire job is prior authorizations for this drug class.”

Caveat: Access friction filters who you hear from — the loudest online voices survived an insurance maze most people never finish. If your journey stalls at the pharmacy counter rather than in your body, that’s a system failure, not a personal one.

The forgotten lunch

“Got buried in work, looked up, and it was 3pm — I hadn’t thought about food once. Not hungry, not craving, nothing. That has never happened in my entire life. I ate a proper lunch anyway because the internet scared me about undereating, and honestly it felt like a chore.”

Caveat: Appetite suppression this strong is exactly why clinicians stress protein targets and scheduled meals on this drug class — forgetting to eat isn’t the goal, and chronic undereating costs muscle. The win isn’t eating nothing; it’s finally being able to eat normally without the noise.

Beyond the scale

“Down 40 lbs, sure — and I’m happy about that. But the things that actually changed my life: my A1c went from 9.1 to 5.8, my knees stopped aching on stairs, I sleep through the night for the first time in a decade, and my blood pressure meds got cut in half.”

Caveat: Metabolic wins are real and they’re measured in the trials too — not just the scale. But joints, sleep, and labs improve with any major weight loss, not uniquely through this drug’s magic. The scale gets the headlines; the quiet health markers do the life-extending.

The maintenance dread

“I’m at goal — 65 lbs down, maintaining for three months. And I am terrified to stop. Everything I’ve read, every story I’ve seen, says it comes back. I don’t want to be on a drug forever, but I’m more scared of watching all of it reverse.”

Caveat: SURMOUNT-4 backs the fear with data — participants switched to placebo regained roughly 14%. This is the honest chronic-therapy conversation: obesity behaves like hypertension, managed rather than cured. If stopping is on your mind, that plan deserves the same medical supervision as starting.
Patterns worth noticing: the “food noise” quieting, escalation-phase GI misery, the 5–8 mg sweet spot, and cost dominating every conversation. None of this is data — but it’s the texture the trials don’t capture.
06Trial dosing

How the trials dosed it

SURMOUNT didn’t start anyone at full strength. Every arm began at 2.5 mg once weekly and climbed 2.5 mg every four weeks — a slow ramp designed to let the gut adapt. What follows is the trial protocol, not a recommendation.

The 5–8 mg sweet spot. Most people don’t need to climb the full ladder: the trials show the bulk of the benefit arriving early — 5 mg already beat semaglutide’s flagship −14.9% — while nausea, vomiting, and dropout rates rise with every step up. Higher doses buy smaller and smaller gains at a higher and higher cost in tolerability.

Dose arms from SURMOUNT-1. Lower arms simply held their dose while higher arms kept climbing.

5 mg −15.0%

Held at 5 mg from week 9. Already past semaglutide’s flagship result — the “low” dose that wasn’t low.

10 mg −19.5% · DIMINISHING RETURNS

Within 1.4 points of the top dose — most of the extra climbing buys little beyond this point, while side effects keep rising.

15 mg −20.9%

The full 20-week ramp to 15 mg. Maximum loss — and the most gastrointestinal side effects.

Plain EnglishJumping straight to 15 mg would be like cannonballing into ice water — the gut rebels. The 4-week steps let nausea peak and fade before the next increase, which is why the worst side effects clustered in the escalation phase and settled later.
Why this chapter exists: the dose arms and the ladder are published trial design — useful context for understanding the results. This page deliberately contains no personal dosing guidance of any kind.
07Risks & unknowns

Risks & open questions

Tirzepatide’s safety profile is the best-characterized of the new incretin drugs — thousands of patient-years across SURMOUNT and SURPASS. The pattern is familiar: gut first, rare-but-serious events behind it, and genuine unknowns beyond that.

Measured in trials

Gut effects dominate

Nausea hit 25–33% during escalation, diarrhea ~19–23%, vomiting ~8–12% — mostly mild-to-moderate and worst while climbing doses. They drove most discontinuations (4–7% of participants).

Measured in trials

Gallbladder

Cholecystitis showed up more on drug than placebo — consistent with rapid weight loss itself, which raises gallstone risk by any method.

Class concern

Pancreatitis

Four adjudicated cases in SURMOUNT-1, spread across groups. Rare — but the class carries the warning, and severe persistent abdominal pain is an emergency symptom.

Class concern

Thyroid C-cell tumors

Rodents developed dose-dependent C-cell tumors; whether it applies to humans is unknown. It’s a boxed warning — personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer rules it out.

Measured in trials

Oral contraceptives

Slower stomach emptying can blunt pill absorption — labeling advises backup contraception for 4 weeks after starting and after each dose escalation.

Known

Small heart-rate bump

Resting pulse rose modestly and dose-dependently: +0.6, +2.3, and +2.6 bpm at 5, 10, and 15 mg (vs +0.1 on placebo) — with a larger transient rise mid-trial that faded by week 72. Small but real, and consistent with the drug class.

Genuinely unknown

DECADES

What 20 years looks like. The longest trials run a few years; lifelong therapy means decades of exposure nobody has observed yet. Every approved chronic drug carries this unknown early on.

PREGNANCY

A data gap, not a green light. Trial participants had to avoid pregnancy; animal studies raised flags. Labeling advises stopping well before a planned pregnancy — real-world data are thin.

RARE EVENTS

Trials can’t catch everything. Even thousands of participants are too few to detect one-in-fifty-thousand harms. That’s what post-market surveillance is for — and why it matters.

Plain EnglishExpect stomach trouble while your dose climbs — that’s the price of admission and it usually settles. Behind it sit the rare-but-serious class risks that are the real reason this is a prescription drug with monitoring, not a supplement. And beyond that sit the honest unknowns no trial has lived long enough to answer.
08Glossary

Words, decoded

Twelve terms you’ll meet everywhere in the tirzepatide conversation — each with the science, a concrete example, and the plain-English version. Tap to expand.

The scienceGlucagon-like peptide-1: an incretin hormone released by L-cells in the lower gut after eating. It slows gastric emptying, suppresses glucagon, stimulates glucose-dependent insulin release, and signals satiety to the brain via the vagus nerve and hypothalamus.

ExampleAfter a big meal, the GLP-1 part of tirzepatide is what makes a second helping feel unappealing — the stomach is still busy with the first.

Plain EnglishYour gut's “we've had enough” text message to your brain and pancreas.

The scienceGlucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide: the gut's other incretin, released by K-cells in the upper intestine. Long thought to promote fat storage, it turned out — when paired with GLP-1 — to amplify insulin release, improve tolerability, and add its own appetite effect. Tirzepatide was engineered as a GIP analog first.

ExampleGIP is the reason many people find tirzepatide's nausea milder than expected from its weight loss — the co-driver smoothing the ride.

Plain EnglishThe gut's second “food's here” hormone — the one that made tirzepatide different from everything before it.

The scienceA single molecule designed to activate two different receptors. Tirzepatide is a dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonist — one drug, two signaling pathways — as opposed to semaglutide (single) or retatrutide (triple).

ExampleOne key that fits two different locks — turning both with a single weekly injection.

Plain EnglishOne drug pulling two levers instead of one.

The scienceA gut hormone released after eating that amplifies insulin secretion in a glucose-dependent way — meaning it boosts insulin when blood sugar is high, but not when it's low (which is why hypoglycemia risk stays modest). GLP-1 and GIP are the two main human incretins.

ExampleIncretins are why eating sugar raises blood glucose less than injecting it would — the gut warns the pancreas dinner is coming.

Plain English“Food's coming” hormones that tell the pancreas to get insulin ready.

The scienceGlycated hemoglobin: the percentage of red-blood-cell hemoglobin with sugar stuck to it — a 2-to-3-month average of blood glucose. Below 5.7% is normal, 6.5%+ is diabetes. Tirzepatide cut HbA1c by about 2 percentage points in the SURPASS diabetes trials, among the largest drops ever seen.

ExampleAn HbA1c falling from 8.5% to 6.4% means average blood sugar dropped from diabetic into near-normal range.

Plain EnglishA 3-month report card for blood sugar, written in your red blood cells.

The scienceTirzepatide's phase 3 obesity trial program: SURMOUNT-1 (flagship weight-loss trial), -2 (with diabetes), -3 (after lifestyle run-in), -4 (randomized withdrawal), -5 (head-to-head vs semaglutide), plus SUMMIT (heart failure). The diabetes program was called SURPASS.

ExampleWhen someone cites “the tirzepatide trials,” they usually mean SURMOUNT-1's −20.9% — but the program answered five more questions after that.

Plain EnglishThe clinical-trial series that proved tirzepatide — one trial per question.

The scienceThe statistical lens SURMOUNT-1 used for its headline numbers: it counts everyone randomized, including people who stopped the drug or missed doses — estimating the effect of being assigned the treatment, not of taking it perfectly. It's the conservative, real-world-flavored estimate.

ExampleIf 100 people were prescribed tirzepatide and 10 quit, the estimand still counts all 100 — so −20.9% already bakes in real-world messiness.

Plain EnglishCounting everyone who started, quitters included — the honest average, not the perfect-patient average.

The scienceThe time for half the drug to clear from the body — about 5 days for tirzepatide, thanks to its albumin-binding fatty-acid tail. After a few weeks, each weekly dose stacks to a steady level; that long tail is also why side effects can linger days after a shot.

ExampleMiss a dose by two days? With a 5-day half-life there's still plenty working — which is exactly why weekly dosing is forgiving.

Plain EnglishHow long half a dose sticks around — long enough that once a week is enough.

The scienceThe satisfied-full feeling that ends a meal — driven by stomach stretch signals, gut hormones (GLP-1, GIP, PYY), and hypothalamic circuits. Tirzepatide amplifies it pharmacologically: smaller meals feel complete, and the “food noise” between meals quiets.

ExampleSatiety is why someone on tirzepatide leaves half a restaurant portion — not from willpower, but because “done” arrives early.

Plain EnglishThe “I'm good, push the plate away” feeling — turned up a notch.

The scienceHow fast the stomach releases food into the intestine. GLP-1 drugs slow it — food sits longer, fullness lasts longer, and post-meal glucose spikes flatten. It's also behind the nausea, the “sulfur burps,” and the oral-contraceptive interaction (pills absorb slower too).

ExampleA meal that used to leave the stomach in an hour now takes three — which is the point, and also the problem.

Plain EnglishPutting a speed bump at the stomach exit — fullness lasts, pills absorb slower.

The scienceTwo brand names, one molecule. Mounjaro: for type 2 diabetes. Zepbound: for chronic weight management and for sleep apnea with obesity. Same tirzepatide inside — different label, indication, and price.

ExampleA pharmacy dispensing Mounjaro vs Zepbound is dispensing the same drug — the difference is what the prescription says it's for.

Plain EnglishSame factory, different can, different shelf.

The scienceEverything that isn't fat: muscle, bone, organs, water. In SURMOUNT-1, fat mass fell 33.9% vs lean mass 10.9% — about a 3:1 ratio, meaning most lost weight was fat. Preserving lean mass (via protein + resistance training) matters because muscle drives metabolism.

ExampleLosing 20 lbs with a 3:1 ratio means ~15 lbs of fat and ~5 of lean — far better than crash diets, but not zero muscle loss.

Plain EnglishThe good stuff you want to keep — muscle and bone — while the fat goes.
09Takeaways

The bottom line

Tirzepatide is the rare drug that lived up to its hype in trials — then kept answering harder questions. Ten things worth carrying away.

🎯

Two levers, proven at scale

The SURMOUNT program tested thousands across six different questions — weight, diabetes, withdrawal, head-to-head, heart failure. Few drugs get examined this thoroughly.

⚖️

The 5–8 mg sweet spot

Most of the benefit arrives early — 5 mg posted −15.0% — while nausea and dropouts rise with every step up the ladder. Higher isn’t automatically better.

🔁

Chronic condition, chronic therapy

SURMOUNT-4: switch to placebo, regain ~14%. Obesity behaves like hypertension — managed, not cured. Plan for maintenance, not a finish line.

💸

Real costs, real risks

GI misery during escalation, gallbladder and boxed-warning risks behind it — and a four-figure monthly price without coverage. Eyes open.

🧪

GIP’s comeback

The hormone everyone wrote off became the key ingredient. Drug discovery rewards the unfashionable idea — today’s dead end is tomorrow’s breakthrough.

🥗

Not a solo act

SURMOUNT-3 showed the drug stacking on top of intensive lifestyle change, not replacing it. The best results in every trial came with food and movement doing their part.

⚔️

The 'zep vs wegovy' wars

Head-to-head tribalism dominates forums — brand loyalties, dose comparisons, side-effect scorecards. SURMOUNT-5 gave the zep side real ammunition.

🏆

SURMOUNT-5 settled one debate

The head-to-head against semaglutide 2.4 mg: tirzepatide won on weight. The argument the internet had been having with anecdotes got an actual answer.

💊

Oral orforglipron is next

The oral small-molecule GLP-1 in development is the post-injection conversation — the field already looking past the needle.

🤢

The nausea curve is real

'It gets better' is the standard forum reassurance — dose-escalation horror stories on one side, smooth rides on the other. Titration is the whole game.
